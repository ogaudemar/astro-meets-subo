Every community has the argument that will not die. Best game in the series, best album, best map, best patch. It runs for forty messages, the loudest three people win, and nothing is settled.

This template settles it. Members rank their top three in order, then say in one line why their number one earned it. You come away with a community ordering backed by average ranks and first-choice counts, plus the actual arguments, which are usually the fun part.

## Why the order matters

A poll collapses everything into one winner. That hides the interesting result: the thing nobody picked first but everybody put second, the two contenders separated by a hair, the item half the community refuses to rank at all.

Ranking keeps all of that. Each item comes back with an average rank, the share of people who put it first, and how many ranked it at all. The bar chart counts first choices, because average rank is an ordering rather than a quantity, and the numbers sit beside it.

## Built so the result is trustworthy

Items are shuffled for every respondent. That is not a cosmetic default: whatever sits at the top of the list gets tapped first, and in a ranking that bias goes straight into the averages you are about to post.

Rankings also have to be complete. Three items, or the whole list, with nothing in between. A half-finished ranking is not a weaker answer, it is a different question, and mixing the two makes every average dishonest.

The worked example is favorite game genres. Swap in Zelda games, Marvel phases, album of the year, your map pool, your community's own running argument.

## Variants

- **Seed a bracket:** rank first, take the top eight by average rank, then run head-to-head polls from the seeding.
- **Rank everything:** drop the top-three limit and have members order all six.
- **Run it every season:** keep the same slate and the movement between runs becomes the story.
- **For creators:** swap the items for content formats or series and it turns into a programming survey instead of a debate.
