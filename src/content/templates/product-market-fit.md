A single satisfaction score tells you *that* something's working, never *what*. The Sean Ellis product-market-fit survey fixes that: it asks how disappointed people would be to lose you — the sharpest leading indicator of fit there is — and then branches so every respondent answers the follow-up that matters for *their* answer.

Users who'd be very or somewhat disappointed tell you the **main benefit** they get, so you know what to market and what to protect. Users who wouldn't be disappointed tell you what would make you a **must-have** — your fit gap, in their words. You finish with two clear, actionable segments instead of one flat number.

## The 40% rule

Sean Ellis found that products where at least 40% of users would be "very disappointed" to lose them tend to have real product-market fit. Below that, you don't yet. This template captures that percentage *and* the reasoning behind it, so the metric comes with a to-do list attached.

## Variants

- **Anonymous mode:** drop the personalization for maximum candor.
- **B2B role screen:** swap the usage question for a role/seniority question when the buyer and the user differ.
- **Add a PMF score:** weight the disappointment answers to trend an aggregate over quarterly runs.
- **Interview recruit:** add a "can we talk more?" question and reward the willing with a badge.
